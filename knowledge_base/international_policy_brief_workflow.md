# International-standard policy brief workflow

## Purpose
The Policy Brief tab is a structured drafting workflow for concise, decision-oriented public policy documents. It replaces the previous fixed narrative that automatically presented dashboard demonstration values as findings.

## Intended users
Policymakers, public-health managers, district and regional teams, researchers, health workers and civil-society organisations.

## Brief structure
1. **Title and document identity** — specific title, geography, intended decision-maker, authoring institution and date.
2. **Key messages** — three to five short, decision-relevant points.
3. **Executive summary** — problem, strongest evidence, proposed action and expected public value.
4. **Policy problem and context** — affected population, scale, urgency, determinants and policy gap.
5. **Evidence and key findings** — sourced findings with units, denominators, dates, geography, methods and disaggregation where available.
6. **Policy options and trade-offs** — include the status quo and feasible alternatives; compare effectiveness, cost, feasibility, equity, acceptability and risks.
7. **Recommended actions** — prioritised, specific actions with responsible actors and timeframes.
8. **Implementation and accountability** — institutional responsibilities, confirmed partners, resource implications, sequencing and delivery risks.
9. **Monitoring, evaluation and learning** — indicators, baseline, target, source, frequency and accountable owner.
10. **Equity, rights and safeguards** — gender, disability, vulnerable groups, human rights, privacy and community engagement.
11. **Limitations and evidence gaps** — uncertainty, missingness, methodological constraints and transferability.
12. **References and sign-off** — traceable citations and recorded technical, legal/policy and approval reviews.

## Generation workflow
- Users enter the problem, decision-maker, geography and evidence; the tool does not infer a policy problem from a dashboard.
- The currently selected dashboard region and country are prefilled as editable context only.
- Dashboard figures are not silently inserted into the brief. The existing prototype uses illustrative data in several areas.
- Empty sections are retained and visibly marked as incomplete rather than fabricated.
- Users can preview and export Markdown or an HTML print view.
- Draft status and review/sign-off fields remain explicit. Downloading a draft is not approval.

## Evidence and data quality rules
For every quantitative claim, record the source/publisher, indicator definition, value, unit and denominator, geography, reference period, method/type (administrative, survey, modelled, projection or other), disaggregation, retrieval/access date and URL/DOI where available.
- Preserve source facets and series identity; do not combine incompatible datasets automatically.
- Do not present national statistics as district, facility or community estimates.
- Do not infer causation from association or trend.
- Distinguish missing data from zero.
- Mark synthetic, illustrative or simulated values prominently and exclude them from evidence-based recommendations.
- If metadata cannot be verified, state that it is not supplied or not verified.
- Use official source definitions rather than generic glossary text where definitions differ.

## Quality assurance checklist before circulation
- Is there one clear policy question and a named decision-maker?
- Does the executive summary state the decision requested?
- Are findings supported by traceable and current sources?
- Are geography, units, denominators and reference periods consistent?
- Are methods, uncertainty, missingness and limitations transparent?
- Are options compared fairly, including costs, feasibility and equity?
- Are recommendations actionable, prioritised and assigned to responsible actors?
- Are implementation resources and risks addressed?
- Are monitoring indicators measurable and linked to a data source?
- Have technical, legal/policy and approval reviews been recorded?
- Is the language understandable to non-specialists?

## International practice alignment
This structure reflects common policy-brief conventions used by public policy and health evidence organisations: concise decision summary, problem definition, evidence, policy options, recommendations, implementation, monitoring and references. It is a good-practice template, not a certification or endorsement by WHO, UN, ITU or another institution. Apply country-specific legal, institutional and publication requirements before release.

## Scope and non-goals
- Existing dashboard, country packs, risk calculations, tabs, UN Data Commons, SDG Navigator, stakeholder workspace, policy deliberation and RAG library remain unchanged.
- This update does not connect new data sources, validate dashboard data, generate automated AI recommendations, or provide legal approval.
- The tool does not generate PDF or DOCX in this iteration.
